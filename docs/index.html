    An AQI reading is a photograph. People with asthma, young children and outdoor workers need the forecast, and the history behind it.

    AERSI multiplies three things: how far pollutants sit above WHO guidelines over a 30-day window, how often the AQI crossed 100, and how violently it swings from one day to the next. A station can post a modest AQI today and still score high because it has been bad for weeks. The forecast side then predicts the next-day AQI for 583 stations and sends an alert to people in cities where it will cross 200.

    Why AERSI?
    AQI is a snapshot. AERSI is a 30-day verdict.

    A clean reading this morning says little about a station that has breached AQI 100 on 20 of the last 30 days. AERSI keeps that history in the score.

    WHO-normalized

    PM2.5 at 15 µg/m³ is the limit, not India's 60. Every pollutant is measured against the WHO 2021 guideline, so a score of 1.0 means "right at the guideline".

    Volatility matters

    A city that swings from 50 to 400 can be harder to adapt to than one steady at 200.

    Methodology

    How one number summarizes a month of air

    AERSI is a product of three factors, each computed per station over a rolling 30-day window. Multiplying them means a station only scores high when it is heavy, persistent and erratic together.

    AERSI=PL0.50×EPF0.25×VSF0.25

    A station sitting exactly at WHO limits, with no days above AQI 100 and no day-to-day swings, scores 1.0.

    01

    Pollution Load

    PL
    PL = Σ wp · (cpWHOp)0.6

    For each pollutant, the concentration is divided by its WHO 2021 guideline, raised to the power 0.6 so that very high readings saturate instead of exploding, and weighted by health burden. If a station does not report a pollutant on a given day, the weights are renormalized over the ones it does report. The daily load is then averaged across the 30-day window.

    Exposure Persistence Factor

    EPF
    EPF = 1 + fraction(days with AQI > 100)

    Measured over the days observed in the 30-day window. The range is 1.0 to 2.0.

    Every day above AQI 100 in the last month adds to the score.

    1.0 never above 1002.0 every day above 100
    03

    Variability Severity Factor

    VSF
    VSF = 1 + tanh(median |ΔAQI|45)

    ΔAQI is the change between consecutive calendar days. Gaps in the data are skipped, so a jump across a missing week is not counted as a daily swing. The range is 1.0 to 2.0.

    Why boundary layer height matters

    Boundary layer height is how deep the lowest layer of air is, the layer pollutants actually mix into. On a calm night it can shrink to a few hundred meters, so the same emissions sit in a thinner layer and concentrations climb. Among the weather inputs it is the highest ranked in this model, though yesterday's AQI still carries most of the signal.
